Executive summary – results
Ten (10) employees were selected to represent similar exposure groups for Personal Noise Exposure Monitoring. The results indicated that six (6) of the monitored personnel were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Excessive Noise Level of 82 dB(A), of which four (4) were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Daily Noise Exposure Limit of 85 dB(A). Meanwhile, four (4) of the monitored personnel were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Maximum Sound Pressure Level of 115 dB(A) and one (1) personnel exceeded the Peak Sound Pressure Level of 140 dB(C). Area monitoring showed sixteen (16) of 25 noise sources above 82 dB(A), the highest being Car Wash – Air Blowgun at 105.5 dB(A). The SEGs of concern are Mechanical (LEX,8h 86.2 dB(A)), Alignment & Balancing (LEX,8h 85.7 dB(A)), Car Washing (LEX,8h 85.0 dB(A)), Painter (LEX,8h 87.0 dB(A)), Paint Mixer (LEX,8h 78.8 dB(A)) and Polisher (LEX,8h 85.6 dB(A)). A Hearing Conservation Programme is therefore required for these groups. Results for personal noise exposure monitoring are summarized in Table 8 whilst the monitoring data are attached in Appendix D and Appendix E.

1.1 Background – appointment & scope
ES Techventure Sdn. Bhd. has been appointed by PCM Klang Motor Sdn Bhd (Toyota Sg. Rasah) to conduct a Review Noise Risk Assessment at their outlet area in compliance with Regulation 4 of the Occupational Safety and Health (Noise Exposure) Regulations 2019. The assessment was carried out by a Noise Risk Assessor registered with the Department of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) Malaysia (Muhammad Salihin bin Mohd Sabri, HQ/21/PEB/00/00040) on 29th & 30th July 2026, when the outlet area was operating under normal conditions. It covered area noise measurement and noise mapping of 25 noise sources and full-shift personal noise exposure monitoring of ten (10) employees representing nine (9) similar exposure groups.

5.3.2 Selection of employees from Similar Exposure Groups (SEG)
Similar exposure groups were identified together with the company representative on the basis of job function, work area and the noise sources each group works with. Nine (9) SEGs comprising 39 workers were identified, from which ten (10) employees (26% of the exposed workforce) were monitored. Within each SEG, the employee expected to receive the highest exposure was selected, i.e. the worker who (a) operates the noise source directly, (b) works near or passes through noisy areas, or (c) spends the longest time in noisy areas. More than one employee was monitored in the Mechanical SEG (2 of 17) because of the larger group size. Selected employees were briefed to carry out their normal duties, so the results represent a typical working day.

7.1 Area noise discussion
Table 7 presents the area noise levels measured at the outlet area, and the noise map is shown in Appendix C. Sixteen (16) of the 25 sources exceeded 82 dB(A), of which fourteen (14) exceeded the NEL of 85 dB(A) and are classified as Red Zone. The dominant sources were Car Wash – Air Blowgun (105.5 dB(A)), Car Wash – Vacuum Cleaner (105.5 dB(A)) and Polish Bay – Air Blow Gun (103.2 dB(A)). At the Service Centre, 6 of 9 sources were in the Red Zone (92.2–105.5 dB(A)). At the Body & Paint, 8 of 13 sources were in the Red Zone (88.0–103.2 dB(A)). At the General Area, levels remained within the Yellow Zone (82–85 dB(A)). Impulsive noise from Knocking was observed; its peak component is better reflected by the personal Peak SPL results in Table 8 than by the area average. Although no workers are stationed there, Air Compressor Room – Air Compressor exceeded 82 dB(A); entry should be limited and PHP worn when entering. All Red and Purple Zone areas shall be demarcated as Hearing Protection Zones with warning signs, and the dominant noise sources are discussed in Table 9.

7.3.5 Adequacy of existing control measures
Existing controls were evaluated against the noise levels measured. The only engineering control observed is that the air compressor is located inside an enclosed room away from the workers; no noise control was observed at the hand tools and pneumatic equipment that dominate exposure, so engineering control is considered inadequate for the SEGs above the NEL. Administrative controls in place include the following: Safe Operating Procedure (SOP) with hearing protection control is available for some high noise activities; Rest area is provided for workers to relieve themselves from occupational noise during breaks. These are supportive but do not on their own reduce exposure below the limits. The unbranded earplugs currently issued have no stated NRR, so their attenuation cannot be verified and they cannot be relied on as a control; effective 1 June 2022 all PHP issued must carry SIRIM-DOSH approval. With the recommended 3M 1271 Reusable Earplugs (NRR 24 dB, derated to 8.5 dB), all monitored employees are estimated to fall below the NEL (LEX,8h 54.9–78.5 dB(A)), Max and Peak limits, so single protection is sufficient when worn consistently. Hearing protection warning signs were observed and should be maintained and extended to all Red Zone areas.

8.0 Recommendations – overview
Based on the results, the following control measures are recommended following the hierarchy of controls and the Hearing Conservation Programme in the Industry Code of Practice for Management of Occupational Noise Exposure and Hearing Conservation 2019. Priority shall be given to the Mechanical, Alignment & Balancing, Painter, Paint Mixer and Polisher SEGs, which exceeded the Noise Exposure Limit. Engineering control at source should target air blow gun, air impact gun, air ratchet and polishing machine, for example silencers or mufflers on pneumatic tools, low-noise air nozzles, repair of compressed-air leaks and enclosure of fixed equipment. Until engineering controls take effect, exposure time should be managed using the daily exposure duration limits in Table 14, and approved PHP must be worn in Hearing Protection Zones. Hearing conservation training and annual audiometric testing are required for the Mechanical, Alignment & Balancing, Car Washing, Painter, Paint Mixer and Polisher SEGs. 9.0 Conclusion
The Review Noise Risk Assessment for PCM Klang Motor Sdn Bhd (Toyota Sg. Rasah) at their outlet area was carried out on 29th & 30th July 2026. Ten (10) employees were selected to represent similar exposure groups for Personal Noise Exposure Monitoring. The results indicated that six (6) of the monitored personnel were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Excessive Noise Level of 82 dB(A), of which four (4) were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Daily Noise Exposure Limit of 85 dB(A). Meanwhile, four (4) of the monitored personnel were exposed to noise levels exceeding the Maximum Sound Pressure Level of 115 dB(A) and one (1) personnel exceeded the Peak Sound Pressure Level of 140 dB(C). Based on the findings, the management shall implement the Hearing Conservation Programme for the Mechanical, Alignment & Balancing, Car Washing, Painter, Paint Mixer and Polisher SEGs, including noise control measures, provision of SIRIM-DOSH approved Personal Hearing Protectors (3M 1271 Reusable Earplugs, NRR 24 dB), annual hearing conservation training and audiometric testing, and demarcation of Hearing Protection Zones. The noise risk assessment shall be reviewed within five (5) years, or earlier when there is any change in process, equipment, work practice or control measures, or when audiometric testing indicates hearing impairment (Regulation 4(5)).
