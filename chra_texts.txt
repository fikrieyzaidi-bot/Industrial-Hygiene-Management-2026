Executive summary – introduction
This is the second Chemical Health Risk Assessment (CHRA) for PCM Klang Motor Sdn. Bhd. (Toyota Body & Paint Kapar) at its workplace located at PT 42315, Jalan Wawasan 1, Kawasan Perindustrian Sungai Kapar Indah, 42200 Klang, Selangor Darul Ehsan. The assessment was carried out by Soo Ee Wah, assisted by Kuan Zhi Yuan and Nur Syazatul (Industrial Hygiene Intern) on 8 September 2026 to identify chemicals hazardous to health (CHTH), evaluate worker exposure and the adequacy of existing control measures, and recommend improvements in compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health (Use and Standards of Exposure of Chemicals Hazardous to Health) Regulations 2000.
Seven (7) work units were assessed covering 168 chemicals. Overall, 5 exposure(s) were rated high risk by inhalation and 19 high risk by skin contact, concentrated in the Panel Beating Technician, Paint Mixer, Spray Painter, Reassembly Technician, General Service Mechanic and Car Wash Operator work units, while most remaining exposures were moderate. The employer is required to act on 15 Action Priority 1 and 25 Action Priority 2 items. The number of chemicals and the risk conclusion for each work unit are summarised below.

4.1 Identification and degree of hazard – discussion
The chemicals in use were identified from the chemical register, Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and container labels. Of the 14 chemicals assessed in detail (work units with full Forms A–D data), the hazard ratings were HR5 (1), HR4 (0), HR3 (4), HR2 (0) and HR1 (0), while 6 were not classified as hazardous to health. Two (2) chemicals are released by the work process (e.g. Metal dust / chips generated from body repair works and Diluted Kleenson Degreaser), and were assessed as they are generated rather than purchased. Of particular concern, WATERMARK® KLEENSON COULOMETRIC VESSEL SOLUTION, DIAPHRAGMLESS, CHLOROFORM-FREE (Car Wash Operator) carries a carcinogenic, mutagenic or reproductive-toxicity classification, so exposure must be reduced as far as reasonably practicable. Two (2) skin or respiratory sensitisers were identified; once a worker is sensitised, even low exposure can trigger a reaction, so skin contact must be prevented. Two (2) corrosive or seriously eye-damaging products (e.g. Kleenson Degreaser) require eye and skin protection during handling.

4.2 Exposure assessment – discussion
Possible routes of exposure were determined for each work unit from the tasks observed and the physical form of the chemicals (Table 4.6). At the Panel Beating Technician work unit, 2 task–chemical combinations were assessed for inhalation and 2 for skin contact; the highest inhalation rating was for Welding fume from welding activities (FDR 3, MR 3, ER 3, RR 15). At the Polisher work unit, 1 task–chemical combination was assessed for inhalation and 3 for skin contact; the highest inhalation rating was for ALPSON A3+ (FDR 4, MR 3, ER 4, RR 12). At the Car Wash Operator work unit, 3 task–chemical combinations were assessed for inhalation and 4 for skin contact; the highest inhalation rating was for WATERMARK® KLEENSON COULOMETRIC VESSEL SOLUTION, DIAPHRAGMLESS, CHLOROFORM-FREE (FDR 4, MR 3, ER 4, RR 20). Ingestion is possible at the Paint Mixer, Spray Painter, Polisher, Reassembly Technician, General Service Mechanic and Car Wash Operator work units where workers were observed eating or drinking near the work area or where hand-to-mouth contact is likely; personal hygiene controls are therefore essential.

4.3 Risk determination – discussion
Overall, 5 exposure(s) were concluded as high inhalation risk, 133 as moderate and 2 as low. For dermal exposure, 19 were high, 132 moderate and 0 low, and ingestion was considered possible for 40 chemical(s). At the Panel Beating Technician work unit, high risk arises from Welding fume from welding activities (inhalation and dermal). At the Car Wash Operator work unit, high risk arises from WATERMARK® KLEENSON COULOMETRIC VESSEL SOLUTION, DIAPHRAGMLESS, CHLOROFORM-FREE (inhalation and dermal), Kleenson Degreaser (dermal) and Diluted Kleenson Degreaser (dermal). The Paint Mixer work unit has 0 high inhalation and 2 high dermal rating(s). The Spray Painter work unit has 0 high inhalation and 5 high dermal rating(s). The Reassembly Technician work unit has 0 high inhalation and 2 high dermal rating(s). The General Service Mechanic work unit has 3 high inhalation and 6 high dermal rating(s). Where risk is high, existing controls are inadequate and priority action (AP1) is required.

4.4.1 Adequacy of technical controls – discussion
Technical controls (isolation, engineering control and ventilation, and PPE) were evaluated for each task. 7 of 9 task–chemical combinations had inadequate overall control, at the Polisher and Car Wash Operator work units. No engineering control was in place for 1 task(s), relying on general or natural ventilation only. Engineering control or ventilation was judged inadequate for 1 task(s). PPE was the most common gap (7 task(s)), mainly the absence of suitable chemical-resistant gloves (e.g. PG Diamond Cut Compound, ALPSON A3+ and SHIN EZ Quick Cutting Compound); PPE provided must be DOSH-approved, suited to the chemical and replaced when worn. Recommended improvements and their action priority are listed in Table 5.3.

4.4.2 Adequacy of organisational controls – discussion
Organisational controls were reviewed against USECHH Regulations 2000 requirements on safe work systems, information, instruction and training, and personal hygiene. 13 of 28 items were found inadequate. The main gaps are that the chemical register is incomplete, some SDS are outdated (older than 5 years) or not available at the work area, chemical containers are not labelled to CLASS Regulations format, no records of safe handling of chemicals training were available, safe work procedures for chemical handling are not established, PPE issuance is not consistently recorded, workers were observed eating or drinking near work areas and USECHH Reg. 29 warning signs are not displayed. These are addressed in Table 5.4 and apply to all work units.

4.4.3 Adequacy of emergency response preparedness – discussion
Emergency response preparedness for chemical incidents was assessed. 2 of 4 items were inadequate: no written spill-handling or medical emergency procedure was available, the emergency reporting flow and fire response plan overlap and may confuse workers and there are no training records for the emergency response team. A written spill-handling and medical emergency procedure, trained emergency responders and periodic spill drills are needed so that workers can respond safely.

5.4 Exposure monitoring – discussion
The need for chemical exposure monitoring was determined from the level of risk, the adequacy of existing controls and whether a permissible exposure limit (PEL) applies. It is not necessary for the Panel Beating Technician, Polisher and Car Wash Operator work units, where exposure is expected to be low and adequately controlled. Details are given in Table 5.7.

5.5 Medical surveillance – discussion
Medical surveillance under USECHH Regulation 27 is required where a chemical listed in Schedule II is used and exposure may affect health, or where exposure monitoring results exceed the PEL. Based on this assessment, medical surveillance is not currently required for any work unit; this shall be reviewed if exposure monitoring results exceed the PEL. Details are given in Table 5.8.

6.0 Conclusion
The CHRA conducted at PCM Klang Motor Sdn. Bhd. (Toyota Body & Paint Kapar) on 8 September 2026 assessed 168 chemicals across seven (7) work units. The risk to health is concluded as SIGNIFICANT for the Panel Beating Technician, Paint Mixer, Spray Painter, Reassembly Technician, General Service Mechanic and Car Wash Operator work unit(s), and the employer shall implement the recommended control measures (15 Action Priority 1 and 25 Action Priority 2 items) within the stipulated timeframe. The employer shall take action within one (1) month of receiving this report (USECHH Reg. 12). This report shall be kept for at least 30 years. Reassessment shall be carried out by September 2031, or earlier if there is a significant change in the work, new chemicals or control measures, adverse health effects are reported, or exposure monitoring results exceed the PEL (USECHH Reg. 10; CHRA Manual Section 4.6).
